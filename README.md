# CCNA-Q

A personal CCNA 200-301 (v1.1) study PWA. It works offline on an iPhone and on the Linux desktop. There's no build step and no backend: plain HTML, CSS and ES modules, served by GitHub Pages.

## What's in it

It's built for the phone first, with desktop as the secondary target. Five thumb-reachable tabs:

| Tab | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Today** | Countdowns (exam Jan 29, v1.1 cutoff Feb 2), tonight's habits, where you are in the book, this week's checklist and theory/practice hours, progress per exam domain, and weak topics. **📅 Plan** opens the full 12-week plan, milestones, hours log, `.ics` export and backup. |
| **Book** | Follows the *CCNA 1 and 2 Companion Guide, 3rd Ed.* chapter by chapter (1–20 + App A). Each chapter has your read/skim/skip triage as a checklist, **⚠ what's out of date** in the 2003 text, a status (not started / reading / done) and a page bookmark. It links to original in-app lessons for that chapter (LingQ-style reader), a **chapter quiz**, **Cornell notes** (cues, notes, summary, plus a self-test mode), matching drills and labs, and the chapter's key terms. There's a mixed 30-question **Book exam** for the Dec 4 milestone. "Beyond the book" holds lessons for v1.1 topics the book lacks; you'll cover those with PC software. |
| **Review** | Spaced repetition of terms you rated 1–4 while reading. Tap to reveal; swipe → good, ← again, ↑ easy. |
| **Drills** | Tap-only quick fire with an on-screen keypad, so the phone keyboard never pops up: subnetting (timed, goal under 30 s), binary/hex conversions, port numbers, OSI layers, administrative distance, CLI modes, router memory, wildcard masks. |
| **Lab** | A stripped-back Packet Tracer (see below). |

**Anki** (Review → Anki ⇄): build decks from the glossary, book quizzes, lesson cues, a facts deck (ports, AD, OSI, masks, IOS modes), your own material (terms, Cornell cues, misses, struggling terms) and custom cards. **Get deck (.apkg)** builds a real Anki package in the browser (sql.js); on iPhone, share it to AnkiMobile. **Send to Anki desktop** pushes through the AnkiConnect add-on and **Pull review stats** brings Anki's lapses back as weak terms. Cards keep stable IDs, so re-importing updates them and keeps your scheduling. Jeremy's IT Lab decks are linked (jitl.jp/ccna-files), not bundled.

**Suggested cues** (Cornell notes → ✨ Suggest cues): leading questions **without answers**, for the cue column. *Quick* uses built-in templates from the glossary terms in the lesson. *AI model* runs a small open model on the device with transformers.js: Qwen2.5-0.5B or SmolLM2-360M, both Apache-2.0. It's free with no key, downloads once from Hugging Face, and then works offline. Every suggestion is cut at its "?" so answers can't slip through.

Reader: every CCNA term is highlighted. **Blue** = new; **yellow** = learning (fades as you rate it 1→4); no highlight = known. Tap a term to rate it. Tap any other word, or select a phrase, to save your own term. Paste in your own text with **Import**. Text size is adjustable (A−/A+).

Wrong answers on quizzes and Cornell self-tests are logged to **Weak topics** automatically.

**Paper notebook ⇄ app** (Book → 📓 Notebook):
- **To write:** the app turns your activity into concrete "write this on page X" items. Quiz, exam and self-test misses become mistake-log lines (p.154). Terms you keep forgetting and terms you saved yourself go to that topic's cue column. Drill and subnetting misses go to the matching reference page. Finished chapters prompt a summary, and finished weeks get a weekly-review row with the numbers filled in. Tick each one off (or swipe it right) once it's on paper.
- **From notebook:** quick capture for things you wrote by hand. Boson/Anki mistakes go to Weak topics, cue questions to Cornell notes and self-test, terms to Review (marked "in my notebook"), and notes to the topic's notes.
- **Index:** notebook pages per chapter and topic, with the planned budget from the guide.
- **Spread preview (PDF):** every chapter can show its notebook pages drawn like the guide (ruled lines, cue column, summary box, corner page numbers), filled with your cue questions (or the lesson's), struggling terms, lab objectives, quiz score and misses, notes and summary, plus a mistake-log page. Save or share it as a PDF; it's generated on the device by `js/pdf.js` (no libraries, works offline).
- Each chapter page has a Notebook panel (quiz best/last vs misses vs what's on paper), Today shows what's waiting, and the Book tab carries a badge.

[`docs/notebook-guide.pdf`](docs/notebook-guide.pdf) is a printable guide to formatting the notebook (generated by `python3 tools/notebook_guide.py`).

Touch details: drag a sheet down to close it, swipe left/right between chapters, haptic ticks where the device supports them, and sheets that stay above the iOS keyboard.

## The mini Packet Tracer

Add PCs, 2960 switches and 4331 routers, then cable them. **Tap** a device for its terminal, **long-press** it for a menu (CLI, ports, cable from here, rename, delete), **drag** to move it, and **pinch** or drag empty space to zoom and pan. Link lights go green or red, and a ping animates the packet along its path.

The terminal is built for thumbs. A row of **suggestion chips** offers the common commands for the current mode, or the valid next words for what you've typed, so you can configure a router mostly by tapping. Keys for ⏎, delete word, Tab, ?, history and `end` sit below.

What the simulated IOS supports:

- Abbreviations (`sh ip int br`, `conf t`, `int g0/0.10`), `?` help, Tab completion, `do`, falling back from sub-modes to global config, `% Invalid input` with the caret
- Interfaces, subinterfaces, loopbacks and SVIs: `ip address`, `shutdown`, `description`, `interface range`
- VLANs, access ports, 802.1Q trunks (native VLAN, allowed list), router-on-a-stick (`encapsulation dot1Q`), `ip default-gateway`
- Static, default and floating static routes, and single-area OSPF (network statements or `ip ospf 1 area 0`, router-id, passive-interface, cost, reference bandwidth, DR/BDR election)
- Standard, extended and named ACLs with `ip access-group in|out` and hit counters
- `show running-config | ip interface brief | ip route | vlan brief | interfaces trunk | mac address-table | arp | cdp neighbors | ip ospf neighbor | access-lists | ip protocols | interfaces`
- Basic hardening: `enable secret` (the password prompt really works), `banner motd`, `line vty`, `username`, `ip domain-name`, `crypto key generate rsa`, `copy run start`
- PCs: `ipconfig <ip> <mask> [gw]`, `ping`, `tracert`, `arp -a`, plus a GUI IP config tab

There are eight guided labs with auto-checked objectives (switch basics, VLANs and trunks, router-on-a-stick, static routes, OSPF, standard and extended ACLs) and an empty sandbox.

It doesn't model STP, DTP, EtherChannel, NAT, DHCP, IPv6 forwarding or timers. Use real Packet Tracer on the desktop for those.

## Deploying (one-time)

1. Push to the default branch (`main`).
2. In the repo, open **Settings → Pages → Build and deployment → Source** and choose **GitHub Actions**.
3. The `Test & deploy` workflow runs the tests and publishes the site to `https://<user>.github.io/ccna-q/`.

Each deploy stamps the service worker with the commit SHA. The app then shows a "new version" bar and you tap Reload.

## Installing

- **iPhone:** open the URL in Safari → Share → **Add to Home Screen**. It runs full-screen and offline.
- **Linux (Chrome/Chromium):** open the URL and use the install icon in the address bar.

Progress is stored in `localStorage` on each device. Use **Plan → Your data → Export/Import** to back up or to move progress between the phone and the desktop. iOS may clear data for home-screen apps that go unused for a few weeks, so export now and then.

## Development

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8000   # then open http://localhost:8000
npm test                      # simulator, labs, subnet generator, SW file list
```

Layout:

```
index.html, manifest.webmanifest, sw.js
css/app.css
js/app.js            router + nav + iOS keyboard handling
js/gestures.js       swipe, long-press, drag-to-dismiss, haptics
js/keypad.js         on-screen keypad for drills
js/drills.js         drill generators (binary/hex, ports, OSI, AD, CLI, memory, wildcards)
js/sync.js           paper notebook ⇄ app bridge (to-write list, capture from paper)
js/store.js          localStorage state, backup
js/srs.js            statuses + SM-2 scheduler
js/terms.js          term matcher/tokenizer for the reader
js/subnetq.js        subnetting question generator
js/data/             glossary, book (chapters, guides, quizzes), lessons, 12-week plan
js/views/            today, book, notebook, read, quiz, notes, review, drill, subnet, lab, plan
js/sim/              net utils, engine (L2/L3), IOS CLI, labs
tests/               node --test suites
```

Chapter lessons live in `js/data/booklessons.js` and topic lessons in `js/data/lessons.js`. Chapter guides and quizzes are in `js/data/book.js`, and vocabulary is in `js/data/glossary.js`. Lessons pick up glossary terms automatically.
